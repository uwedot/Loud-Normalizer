How Saltwell works

Saltwell only generates passwords. It does not save, sync or recover them: once you reload or close the page, a password is gone. Copy it into your password manager or the sign-up form before you leave.

Every choice comes from your device's cryptographic random generator, the same operating-system source on desktop and mobile, with rejection sampling so no character is favoured. Saltwell adds no randomness of its own, so a password is exactly as strong as your device's own random generator.

A statistical self-test runs on load, and every block of random numbers is health-checked as it is drawn. A block that repeats the previous one, a run of identical bytes, or a byte value that turns up far too often stops generation instead of producing a password. These checks catch a broken generator; they cannot prove a working one is secure.

Random passwords always include at least one character from each type you switch on. Saltwell redraws any password that misses a type, and the entropy shown is worked out for exactly that set of results. Strength is log2 of the number of equally likely results your settings can produce. Crack times assume 10¹² guesses per second offline and 10 per second online, and real attackers vary.

Nothing is sent to a server, and the page loads no outside fonts or scripts. Only your option settings are remembered in this browser; generated passwords are never written to storage. Saltwell has not had an independent security audit, so keep what you generate in a reputable password manager.
