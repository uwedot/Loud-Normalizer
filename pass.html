How Saltwell works

Every choice comes from your device's cryptographic random generator, the same operating-system source on desktop and mobile, with rejection sampling so no character is favoured. Rules such as "use every type" are applied by discarding a whole password and drawing a fresh one, never by editing it, so every allowed password is equally likely. Saltwell adds nothing else to it, so a password is exactly as strong as your device's own random generator.

A statistical self-test runs on load, and every block of random numbers is checked for a stuck generator. If either check fails, Saltwell stops instead of producing a password. These checks catch a broken generator; they cannot prove a working one is secure.

Strength is log2 of the number of equally likely results your settings can produce, counted exactly, with the cost of your rules already subtracted. Hex, Base32, Crockford and Base64url tokens are encoded from whole random bytes, and Hex, Base32 and Base64url decode back to exactly those bytes. Base58, Base62 and the prefixed key draw every character uniformly from its alphabet. Crack times assume 10¹² guesses per second offline and 10 per second online, and real attackers vary.

Nothing is sent to a server, and the page loads no outside fonts or scripts. Option settings stay in your browser; passwords are never saved. Saltwell has not had an independent security audit, so for your most important accounts, store the result in a reputable password manager.
